Beginner & Everyday User Guide
Is DirectCrate safe and private? Does it use cloud servers?
DirectCrate is 100% on-device and offline-first. Your own device (phone, Steam Deck, or PC) connects directly to the download link and unpacks files straight into your chosen local folder. No external cloud servers, proxies, or remote middlemen ever touch, see, or store your files or links. There are zero ads, zero user accounts, zero trackers, and zero analytics.
How does DirectCrate save half my storage space?
Traditional download apps force a "double storage penalty": if you download a 10 GB .rar game, your device must store the 10 GB archive file first, and then needs another 10 GB of free space to unpack it—requiring 20 GB of free storage in total! DirectCrate decompresses data in memory as it downloads and writes directly to your final folder. A 10 GB archive only needs 10 GB of storage total, saving half your storage space and leaving zero leftover archive files to delete.
Why does my phone or handheld get warm and use more battery?
Downloading and unpacking massive files live at high speed is intensive work. DirectCrate runs all 4 major hardware systems simultaneously:
  • Network: Downloading gigabytes of data over Wi-Fi or cellular.
  • RAM (Memory): Holding active streaming chunks in working memory.
  • CPU (Processor): Uncompressing complex archive formats (like RAR5 or 7Z) in real time.
  • Storage: Writing thousands of individual files directly to your SSD or flash drive.
This workload is very similar to running a modern 3D game—moderate warmth and battery usage are completely normal and safe. You can check the live App RAM and Device Temp indicators at the bottom of the screen anytime.
Should I use "Unlimited" speed or set a "Speed Cap"?
It depends on your current situation:
  • Unlimited (Full Speed): Downloads and unpacks as fast as your internet connection and device can go. Ideal when plugged into a wall charger or on a desktop PC.
  • Speed Cap (Cool & Battery Saver): Setting a speed cap (such as 10 MB/s or 25 MB/s) from the speed dropdown limits how fast data flows. This gives your device breathing room, lowers CPU usage, keeps temperatures significantly cooler, reduces fan noise on handhelds, and preserves battery life when on the go.
What kind of links can I download? (Direct Link vs. Webpage)
DirectCrate requires a direct file download link—one that immediately starts sending the archive file when clicked (usually ending in .rar, .zip, .7z, .tar.gz, etc., or providing a direct byte stream). DirectCrate cannot download from webpage links (such as file-sharing websites with countdown timers, pop-up ads, "Wait 30 seconds" buttons, or CAPTCHA puzzles). To get a direct link: in your web browser, click the download button so the download begins, then open your browser's download manager, right-click the file, and choose "Copy download link" (or right-click the final button and select "Copy Link Address").
How do I download multi-part archives (part1.rar, part2.rar)?
Many large games and datasets are split into several parts (game.part1.rar, game.part2.rar, game.part3.rar). In DirectCrate, simply switch the mode toggle from Single URL to Multi-Part URLs. Paste all your part links into the box (one link per line or separated by spaces), choose your destination folder, and click Download. DirectCrate will automatically download each part in order, seamlessly stitching them together into one complete, ready-to-use folder.
What if an archive is protected with a password?
If an archive requires a password (common with .7z and .rar archives), DirectCrate detects the encryption before extraction begins. A clean password prompt will appear on your screen. Simply enter the password once, and DirectCrate will decrypt and extract all files automatically.
Why does the download speed go up and down while downloading?
Temporary dips in download speed are completely normal and part of healthy storage operation:
  • When an archive reaches folders containing thousands of tiny files (such as icons, scripts, shaders, or small textures), the operating system must create and register thousands of individual directory entries on the storage drive, which takes a moment and causes the download speed to briefly dip.
  • As soon as the stream moves on to larger files (like 3D models, textures, or video cutscenes), the speed immediately shoots back up to your maximum connection speed.
Can I download in the background? What happens if connection drops or I abort?
  • Background Downloads: Yes! On Android, you can press Home, switch to other apps, or turn off your screen while downloading (just keep the notification visible in your notification shade and do not swipe-kill the app).
  • Connection Drops: If your Wi-Fi or cellular network drops, DirectCrate safely pauses without deleting any files already unpacked. Once your connection returns (or if the link expired and you provide a refreshed link), it resumes right where it left off.
  • Abort Button: If you change your mind and click the red Abort button, DirectCrate immediately stops all network downloads and cleanly deletes any unfinished files so no leftover clutter remains on your storage.
Why did Windows or Android show an "Unknown App" warning and ask for storage permission?
  • Unknown App / SmartScreen Warning: DirectCrate is an independent, free open-source tool downloaded directly from GitHub rather than a commercial app store. Because it is distributed independently, Windows SmartScreen and Android display an "Unknown App" warning on first install. DirectCrate is completely transparent, open-source, and has zero malware or trackers.
  • Storage Permission on Android: Android requires the "All files access" (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) permission so DirectCrate can create your extracted game or application folder inside your public Download directory or on your micro-SD card. DirectCrate strictly writes to your selected folder and never reads, scans, or uploads your personal photos, messages, or files.
Technical & System Architecture
What is zero-temp-disk streaming?
Traditional download managers write a full compressed archive (.rar/.zip) to disk, requiring 2x storage space before unpacking. DirectCrate streams chunks over HTTP Range directly into memory buffers, decompresses on the fly, and writes unpacked files straight to their destination without ever saving intermediate archive files.
Which archive formats and volume chains are supported?
DirectCrate supports single and multi-part RAR5 (.part1.rar, .part2.rar, .r00), standard ZIP archives, 7-Zip (.7z) with AES-256 password decryption, and compressed tarballs (.tar, .tar.gz, .tar.zst) across single or chained URLs.
How does Selective File Inspection work?
For ZIP and 7Z archives, clicking Inspect queries the remote Central Directory or 7Z header via byte ranges without fetching the entire archive. You can inspect the complete file hierarchy and uncheck files you do not need (such as redundant language packs or bonus OSTs) so DirectCrate downloads only selected ranges.
How does expiring CDN link hot-swapping work?
When direct download links expire mid-stream with HTTP 403 Forbidden or 410 Gone, DirectCrate cleanly pauses the stream without discarding partially extracted files. Pasting a renewed direct link resumes the download from the exact byte offset.
How do live progress bar notifications work?
When "Live progress notifications" is enabled in Preferences, DirectCrate provides real-time progress updates across platforms: on Android, it updates the foreground notification with an active progress bar and speed readout; on Linux (KDE Plasma, Bazzite, GNOME), it emits D-Bus Unity.LauncherEntry signals via gdbus; on Windows, it renders green/yellow taskbar progress on the standalone window via ITaskbarList3. Disabling this toggle clears the taskbar bar and keeps the Android notification static.
How does sleep prevention and overnight automation work?
While active, DirectCrate prevents sleep via systemd-inhibit on Linux, thread execution state on Windows, and PowerManager WakeLock / WifiLock on Android. You can configure Post-Completion Power Actions (Sleep or Shut Down with a 15-second grace countdown) which trigger only after the entire batch queue drains.
How does Add to Steam work?
DirectCrate parses your local Steam user directories, backs up shortcuts.vdf to shortcuts.vdf.bak, registers chosen game executables, and automatically queries public Steam Store APIs to fetch vertical capsule posters, horizontal hero banners, and transparent logos into Steam's grid directory.
What are the folder conflict and root flattening policies?
When an extraction folder already exists, DirectCrate can Prompt Every Time, Merge with Existing, Clean & Overwrite, or Auto-Rename with a suffix (_2). Redundant wrapper folders (e.g. Game/Game/game.exe) are automatically hoisted into a clean single folder upon completion.
How does single-instance locking and background lifecycle management work?
On Desktop (Linux & Windows), launching DirectCrate while an instance is already running sends a POST /api/focus signal to the running server to instantly focus or re-open the existing app window without duplicate background processes or port conflicts. When all browser tabs or app windows close, DirectCrate checks whether an extraction or batch queue is active: if active, it continues streaming in the background without interruption; once completely finished and idle with zero connected UI clients for 10 seconds, it gracefully exits. On Android, DirectCrate runs a Foreground Service with WakeLock/WifiLock: swiping the app from Recents while a job is active keeps streaming in the background with notification shade progress; once the queue finishes, it posts a completion notification and automatically stops the service after 5 seconds to conserve battery. If closed while idle, the service stops after a 2.5-second grace period.
How does pre-download validation and low-disk auto-pausing work?
Before initiating any network stream, DirectCrate validates destination directory write permissions via an ephemeral probe file test and checks available filesystem capacity against the archive's remote Content-Length with a 1 GB safety margin. If disk space is exhausted mid-stream (ENOSPC) or write permission is denied (EACCES), DirectCrate safely pauses into needs_disk state without terminating or losing extracted files. It automatically polls the target directory every 2 seconds and resumes extraction the instant at least 512 MB of free space is cleared.
How does archive path sanitization protect against directory traversal?
All entry paths within RAR, ZIP, 7Z, and TAR archives undergo strict cross-platform sanitization. Any entry containing relative path traversal components (../) is rejected immediately. Backslashes are normalized, redundant dot segments are collapsed, and characters forbidden on Windows, NTFS, or Android FAT/exFAT filesystems (such as :, *, ?, ", <, >, |, and trailing dots/spaces) are stripped or replaced so archives unpack cleanly on any operating system.
What happens if configuration or history files become corrupted?
If sudden power loss, system crash, or interrupted I/O corrupts settings.json or history.json, DirectCrate's startup engine traps the syntax error, preserves the damaged file as <filename>.corrupt.bak for diagnostic inspection, logs a warning, and cleanly loads valid defaults without failing to launch.
How does the Android 15/16 fail-safe bootstrap and one-tap recovery work?
On Android 15 and 16, DirectCrate starts the native Go engine directly on a background thread with thread-safe idempotent port detection, guaranteeing loopback availability even if modern background foreground-service start restrictions delay the service. It validates All Files Access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) for public Download and SD card extraction, polls the status endpoint with an automatic 3-second re-invocation safeguard, and presents an interactive "Retry Connection" button on the error screen if startup encounters a delay.
How does DirectCrate manage device hardware resources (Network, RAM, CPU, Storage)?
DirectCrate is engineered for extreme hardware efficiency with full transparency across 4 core subsystems:
  • Network: Streams directly over HTTP/HTTPS with parallel HTTP Range prefetching (1–8 connections, default 4), token-bucket bandwidth limiting (5–50 MB/s or Unlimited), persistent TCP connection pooling, and seamless resume for dropped connections or expired CDN URLs.
  • RAM: Consistently operates with a tiny memory footprint (typically 30–64 MB) displayed live in the telemetry bar. Buffers are recycled through reusable pools (sync.Pool) in 256 KB chunks, preventing garbage collection stalls and out-of-memory errors on 4 GB–16 GB devices.
  • CPU: Native multi-format streaming decompressors (RAR5, ZIP, 7Z, TAR.GZ, TAR.ZST) run at normal process priority without maxing out CPU cores or overheating handhelds, preserving battery life and keeping device thermals cool.
  • Storage: Zero intermediate archive files! Decompressed files are written straight to destination storage with pre-flight write probes, 1 GB capacity checks, and mid-stream needs_disk auto-pausing with a 512 MB resume floor.
What data is stored locally and what telemetry is sent?
DirectCrate is 100% private and offline-first with zero telemetry, zero analytics, and zero tracking. All user preferences, logs, and extraction history reside strictly in ~/.config/directcrate/ (or app-internal storage on Android). External network calls are strictly limited to your provided archive URLs and optional Steam artwork lookups.

Beginner Quick Reference
  • 100% On-Device & Private: Runs entirely on your phone or PC. No cloud servers, proxies, or tracking.

Platform Integrations & OS Hooks
  • Linux / SteamOS / Bazzite: Standalone chromeless app window (Chromium binaries & Flatpaks), D-Bus LauncherEntry taskbar progress, systemd-inhibit sleep lock, and notify-send cues.
  • Windows: Standalone Edge/Chrome app window, ITaskbarList3 taskbar progress bar (normal green / paused yellow), and native system sleep inhibition.
  • Android 15/16: 16KB ELF page-aligned native library (arm64-v8a), direct idempotent background engine bootstrap with foreground service fallback, MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE checks, one-tap connection recovery, and system-synced theming.
  • Smart Lifecycle & Single-Instance: Single-instance desktop lock with 10s idle auto-exit when all windows close; Android foreground service keeps streaming through Recents dismissal and auto-stops 5s after batch completion.
  • Lifecycle & Fault Tolerance: Pre-download write-probe and storage margin checks, mid-stream needs_disk auto-pausing (512 MB resume floor), directory traversal prevention (../ blocking), automatic .corrupt.bak state recovery, and decompression panic recovery traps.
Hardware & Resource Footprint
  • Network: Parallel HTTP Range streaming (1–8 conns), token-bucket speed capping, and auto-resume.
  • RAM: 30–64 MB typical footprint tracked in live telemetry, with zero-leak sync.Pool buffer recycling.
  • CPU: Optimized single/multi-thread streaming decompression designed for handhelds and battery longevity.
  • Storage: Direct-to-disk streaming eliminating the 2x storage penalty of traditional downloaders.
